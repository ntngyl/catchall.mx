Mail for every address at your domain, forwarded to the inbox you already use.

Add two DNS records and anything@yourdomain.com lands in your Gmail, Outlook or wherever you read mail. It is free. There is no account to create, and we do not keep your mail.

It reads your public DNS records and tells you what is missing. Nothing is saved. If you would rather be told exactly what to type, use the record builder.

Setting it up

You do this at whoever runs your domain's DNS: Cloudflare, GoDaddy, Namecheap and so on. Both records go on the domain itself, which most providers write as @.

  1. Tell the world we receive your mail. That is an MX record.
  2. Tell us where to send it. That is a TXT record with your real address in it.
MXmail.catchall.mx  priority 10
TXTcatchall-forward=you@example.com
  1. Confirm once. Check your domain in the box above. When the records are right, a button appears that emails a confirmation link to your inbox. Click it and forwarding starts.

The confirmation exists so nobody can point a domain at your address and flood you. If the email is not in your inbox within a minute or two, look in spam and mark it "Not spam".

That is the whole setup. To undo it, delete the two records.

Rules for single addresses

A catch-all accepts everything, which is the point, until one address starts collecting junk. You can deal with that using a few more TXT records. Names are the part before the @.

TXTcatchall-route=billing:accounts@example.com Send billing@ to a different mailbox. That mailbox confirms once, like yours did.
TXTcatchall-block=oldshop,news-* Refuse mail to these. The star matches anything, so news-* covers news-daily, news-offers and the rest.
TXTcatchall-only=info,sales,me The strict option: accept only these addresses and refuse everything else.

A trick worth knowing: give every shop and newsletter its own address, like shoename@yourdomain.com. When that address starts getting spam, you know who leaked it, and you can block just that one.

There are a few more switches, all optional:

Writing these by hand gets fiddly. The record builder asks what you want, looks at what your domain has now, and lists what to add, change or delete, in the wording your DNS provider uses. It can also set up DKIM, so forwarded mail is signed by your own domain.

What happens to your mail

A message arrives. We look up your domain's DNS to see where it goes, run it through a spam filter on our own server, forward it, and delete our copy once your mail provider has accepted it. No part of a message is sent to anyone else.

What we keep

  • That your domain is confirmed, with each confirmed mailbox stored as a fingerprint.
  • A count of emails per domain per day, for the limit.
  • Delivery logs (domain, destination, time) for 7 days.
  • Mail waiting for a mailbox to confirm, for 24 hours.
  • Mail held as a suspected forgery, for 7 days.

What we don't do

  • Read or analyse what your mail says.
  • Add tracking pixels or rewrite links.
  • Sell or share addresses.
  • Track you on this site. There are no analytics or cookies here.
  • Ask for an account, so there is no profile of you to leak.

Questions people ask

Is it actually free?

Yes, and it stays free. There is no paid plan hiding behind it. The daily limit is there to keep one busy domain from slowing everyone else down.

Why do some forwarded emails say "via CatchAll" next to the sender?

Gmail and others reject forwarded mail that cannot prove where it came from. If the original sender signed their message, we pass it on untouched. If they did not, we send it under our own address, keep the real sender's name visible and put their address in Reply-To, so it arrives and your reply still goes to the right person.

Something landed in spam.

Mark it "Not spam" once. Your provider learns quickly from that. Adding forwarded@catchall.mx and verify@catchall.mx to your contacts helps too.

What if I change the forwarding address?

The new mailbox has to confirm before it receives anything. Check your domain again and send the confirmation from here. Mail that arrives in the meantime waits up to 24 hours.

Can I send mail from my domain?

No. This only receives. Replies go out from your own mailbox, under that mailbox's address.

My domain already has mailboxes somewhere.

Then think before you switch. A domain's mail goes to one place, so pointing the MX record here moves all of it to forwarding and those mailboxes stop getting new mail.

Someone is using this to send me mail I never asked for.

Click "Block" in the confirmation email, or report the domain. Domains and mailboxes used for abuse get blocked.
