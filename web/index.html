Setting it upAbout two minutes at your DNS provider.

  1. Tell the world we receive your mail.

    Add an MX record on the domain itself, which most providers write as @.

    MXmail.catchall.mx  priority 10
  2. Tell us where to send it.

    Add a TXT record with the address where you actually read mail.

    TXTcatchall-forward=you@example.com
  3. Confirm once.

    Check your domain at the top of this page. When the records are right, a button appears that emails a confirmation link to your inbox. Click it and forwarding starts. If the email isn't there in a minute or two, look in spam.

That's the whole setup. To undo it, delete the two records. The confirmation exists so nobody can point a domain at your address and flood you.

Rules for single addressesOptional. Still just DNS records.

A catch-all accepts everything, which is the point, until one address starts collecting junk. A few more TXT records deal with that.

Send one address somewhere else

For example, billing@ goes to your accountant. That mailbox confirms once, like yours did.

TXTcatchall-route=billing:accounts@example.com

Refuse an address

The star matches anything, so news-* covers news-daily, news-offers and the rest.

TXTcatchall-block=oldshop,news-*

Accept only the addresses you list

The strict option. Everything else is refused before it is even sent to us.

TXTcatchall-only=info,sales,me

A trick worth knowing. Give every shop and newsletter its own address, like shoename@yourdomain.com. When that address starts getting spam, you know who leaked it, and you can block just that one.

What happens to your mailShort version: it passes through.

A message arrives. We look up your domain's DNS to see where it goes, run it through a spam filter on our own server, forward it, and delete our copy once your mail provider has accepted it.

No part of a message is sent to anyone else.

What we keep

  • Confirmed status of your domain, with each mailbox stored as a fingerprint.
  • A daily count of emails per domain, for the limit.
  • Delivery logs (domain, destination, time) for 7 days.
  • Waiting mail for an unconfirmed mailbox, 24 hours at most.
  • Suspected forgeries, held 7 days at most.

What we don't do

  • Read or analyse what your mail says.
  • Add tracking pixels or rewrite links.
  • Sell or share addresses.
  • Track you on this site. No analytics, no cookies.
  • Ask for an account, so there is no profile to leak.

Questions people ask

Is it actually free?

Yes, and it stays free. There is no paid plan hiding behind it. The daily limit keeps one busy domain from slowing everyone else down.

Why do some forwarded emails say "via CatchAll" next to the sender?

Gmail and others reject forwarded mail that can't prove where it came from. If the original sender signed their message, we pass it on untouched. If they didn't, we send it under our own address, keep the real sender's name visible and put their address in Reply-To, so it arrives and your reply still goes to the right person.

Something landed in spam.

Mark it "Not spam" once. Your provider learns quickly from that. Adding forwarded@catchall.mx and verify@catchall.mx to your contacts helps too.

What if I change the forwarding address?

The new mailbox has to confirm before it receives anything. Check your domain again and send the confirmation from here. Mail that arrives in the meantime waits up to 24 hours.

Can I send mail from my domain?

No. This only receives. Replies go out from your own mailbox, under that mailbox's address.

My domain already has mailboxes somewhere.

Then think before you switch. A domain's mail goes to one place, so pointing the MX record here moves all of it to forwarding, and those mailboxes stop getting new mail.

Someone is using this to send me mail I never asked for.

Click "Block" in the confirmation email, or report the domain. Domains and mailboxes used for abuse get blocked.
