SPF checker

Reads a domain's SPF record one term at a time, says what each part allows, and counts the DNS lookups against the limit of 10.

Reads public DNS only. Lookups are not saved, and there is nothing to sign up for.

How SPF works

SPF is one TXT record on the domain that starts with v=spf1. It lists who may send mail for the domain, then ends with what to do about everyone else:

include:_spf.google.comAllow the servers this other record allows. One per mail service you send through.
ip4:203.0.113.5Allow this address.
mx / aAllow the domain's own mail servers, or its own address.
-allReject everyone not listed.
~allTreat everyone not listed as suspicious. The usual choice.

Common problems

Two SPF records

A domain may have only one. With two, receivers give up and SPF fails for everything. This is the most common SPF mistake: each new service's instructions say "add this TXT record" when they should say "add this include to your existing record".

More than 10 DNS lookups

Every include, a, mx and redirect costs a lookup, and includes can contain more includes. Past 10, receivers stop and return an error. The tool follows the whole chain and gives you the real number.

+all or ?all at the end

+all lets anyone send as your domain. ?all says you have no opinion. Either way the record protects nothing.

SPF and forwarding

When mail is forwarded, it arrives from the forwarder's server, which is not in the original sender's SPF record. That is why forwarded mail depends on DKIM, and why catchall.mx leaves a valid DKIM signature untouched.
