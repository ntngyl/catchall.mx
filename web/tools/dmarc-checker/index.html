DMARC checker

Shows a domain's DMARC record, what each part of it means, and whether forged mail using the domain is stopped or still delivered.

Reads public DNS only. Lookups are not saved, and there is nothing to sign up for.

How DMARC works

DMARC is a TXT record at _dmarc.yourdomain.com. A message passes when SPF or DKIM passes and the domain that passed matches the one in the From line. For everything else, the record's policy applies:

p=noneDeliver as usual. Only collect reports.
p=quarantinePut failing mail in the spam folder.
p=rejectRefuse failing mail.
rua=mailto:…Where receivers send their daily summary reports.
sp=A separate policy for subdomains.

A subdomain without its own record uses the main domain's. The tool follows that and tells you which record applied.

A safe way to turn it on

  1. Start with v=DMARC1; p=none; rua=mailto:you@yourdomain.com. Nothing changes for your mail; you only start getting reports.
  2. Read the reports for a couple of weeks. They list every server sending as your domain and whether it passed.
  3. Fix the services that fail, usually by setting up DKIM for them.
  4. Move to p=quarantine, and later p=reject.

Gmail and Yahoo expect at least p=none from domains that send in volume, so having no record at all is the one state to avoid.

Common problems

The record is on the wrong name

It belongs on _dmarc, not on the domain itself. Some DNS forms add the domain automatically, so typing the full name produces _dmarc.example.com.example.com.

Two DMARC records

With more than one, receivers ignore DMARC for the domain entirely.

No rua address

The policy still works, but you never see who is sending as your domain, which is most of what makes DMARC useful.
